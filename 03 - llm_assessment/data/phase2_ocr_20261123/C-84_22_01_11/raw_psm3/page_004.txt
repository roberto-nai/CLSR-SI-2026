EUROPEAN AND DOMESTIC LEGISLATION

The Environmental Information Directive distinguishes between the “infernal
communications” of a public authority (Article 4(1)(e)), and the “proceedings” of a

public authority (Article 4(2)(a)).

Both Article 4(1)(e) and Article 4(2)(a) of the Environmental Information Directive are

qualified by the second paragraph of Article 4(2), which states:
“The grounds for refusal mentioned in paragraphs | and 2 shall be
interpreted in a restrictive way, taking into account for the particular
case the public interest served by disclosure. In every particular case,
the public interest served by disclosure shall be weighed against the
interest served by the refusal. Member States may not, by virtue of
paragraph 2(a), (d), (f), (g), and (h) provide for a request to be refused
where the request relates to information on emissions into the
environment.”

Relevantly, the mandatory requirement to allow access where the request relates to

information on emissions into the environment does not apply to the “internal

communications” of a public authority.

The Environmental Information Directive has been transposed into domestic law by the
European Communities (Access to Information on the Environment) Regulations 2007

(S.I. No. 133/2007) (28 March 2007).
